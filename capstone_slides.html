Introduction

Introduction

Background

SpaceX advertises a Falcon 9 rocket launch at $62 million, while other providers charge upwards of $165 million. Most of this saving comes from reusing the first stage: SpaceX lands it (on a drone ship or a ground pad) and flies it again instead of losing it in the ocean.

The problem

If the first stage cannot be recovered, the launch costs far more. So the key business question is: can we predict whether the Falcon 9 first stage will land successfully? If we can predict the landing outcome, we can estimate the cost of a launch.

What this project does

  • Collects Falcon 9 launch data from the SpaceX API and Wikipedia (90 launches, 2010–2020).
  • Explores which factors affect landing success: launch site, orbit, payload mass, flight number and landing outcome.
  • Builds an interactive dashboard and map of launch sites and outcomes.
  • Trains machine-learning models to predict landing success (Class = 1) vs. failure (Class = 0).

Data Wrangling

Data Wrangling Methodology

  • Missing values: only LandingPad has missing values (26 of 90); all other columns are complete. PayloadMass mean is 6,105 kg (min 350 kg, max 15,600 kg).
  • Outcome labels: the Outcome column (e.g. True ASDS, True RTLS, None None) was converted into a binary target Class — 1 = first stage landed successfully, 0 = otherwise. Overall success rate: 66.7% (60 of 90).
  • One-hot encoding: Orbit (11 categories), LaunchSite (3), LandingPad (5) and Serial (53) were one-hot encoded, giving 80 feature columns for modelling.
  • Result: dataset_part_2.csv — 90 rows × 18 columns (dataset_part_1.csv + Class).

Conclusion

Conclusion

  • Landing success is predictable. A Decision Tree predicts first-stage landing success with 94.44% test accuracy, so the cost of a Falcon 9 launch can be estimated before flight.
  • Experience matters most. Success rose from 0% in 2010–2013 to 90.0% in 2019 as SpaceX gained flight experience.
  • Site and orbit matter. KSC LC 39A is the most reliable site (77.3%); VLEO (85.7%) and SSO (100%) are the most reliable orbits, GTO the hardest (51.9%).
  • Payload is not the deciding factor. Landings succeed across the full 350–15,600 kg payload range.
  • Reuse drives the cost advantage: recovering the first stage is what makes a $62M Falcon 9 launch possible.
